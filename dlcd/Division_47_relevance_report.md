# Method

I reviewed the DLCD public-comment compilation (203 pages), the six
published Phase One RAC meeting summaries, and DLCD's \*\*August 6, 2026
Version 4\*\* Division 47 draft. "Relevant" means the comment bears
directly on one or more facts identified in the supplied personal
record: limited income/caregiving work; disability/health vulnerability;
foreclosure/title dispute; utility/water and waste-service interruption;
code-enforcement consequences; risk that an occupied or recently lost
dwelling is converted into a "vacant" redevelopment site; accessibility;
and meaningful lived-experience participation.

I did \*\*not\*\* treat every comment mentioning "utility," "trash," or
"vacant" as support for your position. Several selected comments support
the underlying operational need for safe, reliable infrastructure, but
urge broader local discretion. They are useful for showing that service
access, waste collection, pedestrian access, and utility easements are
recognized Division 47 subjects---not necessarily for endorsing their
requested policy outcome.

# Your public comment: complete relevant text

\*\*Annika Eriksson, submitted July 7, 2026; public-comments compilation
pages 3--6 (PDF pages 5--8).\*\*

Your comment is the central record item. It asks DLCD to:

1\. \*\*Amend OAR 660-047-0010(6)(h) / "Vacant"\*\* so a lot is not
treated as vacant where the prior residence was lost through municipal
utility termination, code-enforcement action, or lien foreclosure within
10 years, and where title disputes, municipal appeals, or litigation
seeking to retain the home remain active.

2\. \*\*Amend OAR 660-047-0020(7)\*\* so "sufficient infrastructure"
requires continuous, nondiscriminatory utility and waste service to
existing residents---including people experiencing severe
hardship---rather than merely system capacity for new development.

3\. \*\*Amend OAR 660-047-0030(3)\*\* to create an exemption when a
household is denied franchised waste collection because of hardship
through no fault of its own, or require universal waste-service access
and reasonable payment plans before applying the storage standard.

4\. Add a \*\*socioeconomic equity, legal-vulnerability, and
due-process\*\* component to the impact-statement framework.

The source text is preserved in the selected-pages packet.

# RAC record relevant to your situation

## 1. Equity framework repeatedly identified disability, access, safety, and housing insecurity

Across Meetings 1--6, DLCD's RAC record repeatedly states that people
with disabilities are disproportionately housing insecure; that
administrative and policy barriers can delay housing and increase costs;
that siting/design affect safety and wellbeing; and that accessible
housing and cooperative/community models should be considered. Meetings
2 and 3 further note that site access to buildings is within DLCD's
scope even if interior unit design is primarily a building-code issue.

\*\*Use:\*\* Cite this recurring RAC theme to show that a practical
access-and-displacement analysis is consistent with the process DLCD
itself framed as equity-centered.

## 2. Meeting 1 --- February 18, 2026

- The RAC's stated equity questions asked how siting/design affect
  people by income and disability, which groups have held influence, and
  what foreseeable consequences the rules may create.

- Comments recognized that housing costs and inventory matter for
  lower-income households; older neighborhoods with missing sidewalks
  create safety barriers; and utility infrastructure can delay housing.

- DLCD stated that the Phase One deadline was statutory, but the
  racial-equity framework would be used throughout the rulemaking.

\*\*Use:\*\* Supports asking DLCD to add a concrete "existing residents
/ service continuity / displacement" assessment rather than treating
equity as only a production-side question.

## 3. Meeting 2 --- March 11, 2026

- RAC members elevated the need for wheelchair-accessible housing and
  access to buildings. DLCD responded that building access on the site
  was within scope.

- The meeting summary says safety and prevention of future displacement
  were examples of standards for which variances may not be appropriate.

\*\*Use:\*\* Your proposed service-continuity safeguard can be framed as
a \*\*site/housing-stability\*\* protection, not an
interior-building-code request.

## 4. Meeting 3 --- April 1, 2026

- RAC members again raised ADA access in lot design and access to

  Oregon Homes are built, rather than interior construction.

\*\*Use:\*\* Supports a narrowly tailored condition: prevent rules from
classifying homes as redevelopment-ready where municipal actions have

## 6. Meeting 5 --- May 20, 2026

\*\*Use:\*\* Direct support for your requested

# Suggested framing for a follow-up comment

Keep the submission tightly tied to Division 47's stated purpose:
expedited approval of qualifying housing \*\*must not create an
incentive or pathway for a local government to make existing homes
unlivable, classify the resulting site as vacant, and then count
redevelopment as housing progress.\*\*

A concise formulation:

> Division 47 should distinguish true vacant land from a site made
> "vacant" by displacement. The current draft regulates new-housing
> approval, utilities, waste storage, and lot eligibility, but does not
> prevent municipal or franchised-service actions from converting an
> occupied, low-income, disabled, or legally vulnerable household's home
> into redevelopment eligibility. The RAC record repeatedly identifies
> disability, affordability, safety, and housing insecurity as equity
> concerns, and the July 9 summary records that lived experience of
> instability received insufficient attention. DLCD should add an
> objective anti-displacement safeguard to the definition of "vacant,"
> public-facilities review, and impact analysis.

# Limits and verification notes

  necessarily a complete archive of every submission or attachment DLCD
  received. This review covers the comments that appear in that
  compilation through the document's latest listed material (August
  2026).

- The published RAC minutes generally attribute remarks to "a RAC
  member" rather than naming the speaker. Therefore, the minutes confirm
  \*\*that a point was raised\*\*, but normally do not prove that any
  particular remark was yours.

- The supplied personal record describes serious disputes and harms. I
  have treated these as \*\*your reported circumstances for relevance
  analysis\*\*, not independently adjudicated facts.
