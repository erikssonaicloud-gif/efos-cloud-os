Section 1 & 2 · Executive Synthesis

Abstract & Evidentiary Classification Table

Abstract. This study reviews published Oregon City records (1,930 pages of municipal and state rulemaking packets, 165 historical Commission minutes, and federal and state court filings) and twelve USGS Landsat Collection 2 Level-2 surface-temperature scenes (1994–2024) to examine how the City administers utility billing, disability-access processes, code enforcement, and neighborhood surface-temperature conditions. Findings are classified by evidentiary strength — verified in an inspected record, reproduced by this project's computational pipeline, documented variance, or unresolved question — so that each claim can be weighed independently. Four principal findings are reported. (1) A February 3, 2026 Public Works memorandum states that the Municipal Code establishes no formal appeal process for water or sanitary sewer charges; an internal policy describes a 30-day written review window, and the record does not yet show what notice of that window was given in any individual case. (2) The City's August 19, 2026 ADA Transition Plan is limited to physical barriers in the public right-of-way. (3) Commission records show individualized forbearance on certain right-of-way encroachments within a generally complaint-driven enforcement model; the comparison involves different property types and is not yet established as a pattern. (4) Surface temperatures at Chapin Court exceeded two comparison sites on all twelve sampled dates (+7.0°F to +17.3°F). The contrast is persistent, but the data do not identify its cause, do not show that it widened after the 2009–2018 period, and — because Landsat thermal bands are natively 100–120 m and resampled to 30 m — cannot support parcel-scale attribution. This is an independent, self-published study. It is not peer-reviewed, not a certified expert report, and not a finding of wrongdoing.

Workstream 1 · Municipal Public Works & Finance

Utility Billing, Disconnection Governance, and Financial Assistance

Mirroring Sections 3–6 of the Latvian Ķekavas nami utility study, Workstream 1 separates (a) codified municipal ordinance authority, (b) internal administrative billing policy, (c) unverified account-level duration records awaiting primary ledger registration, and (d) federal infrastructure financing.

1.1 Codified Appeal Absence, Internal 30-Day Review Window & 180-Day Payment Terms Class A-V · Verified Record & Policy Text

In the February 10, 2026 City Commission Work Session packet (S1, CivicClerk Event 3240, File 8700, pp. 20–27), Public Works Director Dayna Webb presented the February 3, 2026 memorandum Utility Service Charges Code Consistency Review. Evaluating water, sanitary sewer, stormwater, and pavement maintenance charges billed on a combined monthly statement, the memorandum states on the official record: “The Code does not establish a formal appeal process” for water and sanitary sewer charges (Class A-V, direct quotation from inspected record), noting that staff rely on “interpretation or past practice” and that combined utility billing has inconsistent collection, lien, assistance, and appeal rules across services.

The accompanying matrix on packet page 27 and policy summary on pages 25–27 identify two distinct internal provisions that must not be conflated: first, an internal 30-day written review window to the Finance Director/City Manager inside an internal Utility Billing Policy (Class A-V for the policy text; what notice of that window was given in practice in any individual case remains unknown); and second, 180-day payment arrangements (Class A-V for the policy text; this is a payment-plan term, not a cap on disconnection duration), alongside an income-based reduced-rate program cutting base charges by 50% and water consumption charges by approximately 20%.

1.2 Federal WIFIA Award ($9.29M) vs. Unproven § 504 Departmental Scope Class A-V (WIFIA Contract) · Class B (§ 504 Scope)

Two additional primary packets examine institutional billing flexibility and federal financial assistance. First, in the May 15, 2024 Commission packet (S4, pp. 46–53, proposed Resolution 24-16), staff recommended a sewer-billing methodology adjustment for Clackamas Community College (whose irrigation water did not enter the sanitary sewer), reducing annual sewer charges from $62,528 to $25,657 (an estimated $36,900/year reduction; Class A-V for the record text, though an institutional irrigation account is not a matched residential comparator). Second, the August 19, 2026 packet (S2, pp. 80–85) documents a $9,291,680 ($9,295,710 staff recommendation) construction award for the Henrici Water Transmission Main funded under the City's federal EPA WIFIA loan (Class A-V, contract and staff report). However, whether Rehabilitation Act § 504 program coverage extends across Public Works utility billing and disconnection administration remains Class B (departmental scope unproven; Priority 4) until the executed WIFIA loan agreement, federal civil-rights assurances, and departmental accounting records are inspected.

Evidentiary Classification of the 509-Day Disconnection Duration (Class A-R or C — Not Class B) • 509-Day Disconnection Duration (June 25, 2024 – November 16, 2025): Classified as A-R or C — not B. Workstream 2 · Civil Rights Governance & Rulemaking Participation

ADA Title II Scope Boundaries, Historical Public Voices & State Rulemaking

Workstream 2 examines the documentary relationship between published disability-access plans, administrative grievance structures, comparator federal pleadings, and 13 years of recorded resident testimony (2013–2026).

2.1 The August 2026 ADA Transition Plan Right-of-Way Scope & Grievance Routing Class A-V · Verified Plan & Policy Text

In the August 19, 2026 Commission packet (S2, pp. 27–62), Assistant City Manager Alexandra Troutman and Public Works Operations Manager Josh Wheeler presented the City's Self-Evaluation and ADA Transition Plan. While noting Title II obligations to evaluate public services, policies, and practices, the plan explicitly limits its scope to physical barriers in the public right-of-way and states that other City facilities, programs, and administrative services are outside its evaluation (Class A-V, Plan text). Concurrently, the City's published ADA Grievance Procedure webpage (S5) assigns final administrative appeal authority over service-access grievances to the City Manager or designee (Class A-V).

Contemporaneous municipal records in the July 17, 2024 Commission packet (S3, pp. 3, 16, 28, 48–49) pair a Disability Pride Month Proclamation with Natural Hazard Mitigation Plan updates ranking Extreme Heat #2 in the local hazard matrix (Risk Score 220, High Probability / High Vulnerability) and noting Public Works' role in potable water delivery during heat emergencies (Class A-V).

Workstream 3 · Independent Geospatial & Atmospheric Investigation

Environmental Forensics: Twelve USGS Landsat Surface-Temperature Scenes (1994–2024), Spatial Resolution Limits & AQI Audit

Examined as a separate environmental investigation across three internal audit cycles (Cycle 1 Litigation-Readiness Critique, Oct 19, 2026; Cycle 2 USGS Collection 2 Level-2 Raster Pipeline Reproduction, Oct 28, 2026; and Cycle 3 Weather Provenance, 3×3 Spatial Buffer & CAMS AQI Reanalysis, Nov 2, 2026).

3.1 Methods, Native Sensor Resolution (100–120 m Resampled to 30 m) & Pipeline Reproduction of 12 Landsat Scenes Class A-R · Pipeline Reproduced (Internal Consistency Only)

Using the Microsoft Planetary Computer STAC API (landsat-c2-l2) and the USGS Collection 2 Level-2 surface-temperature scaling formula ($K = \text{DN} \times 0.00341802 + 149.0$; $^{\circ}\text{F} = (K - 273.15) \times 1.8 + 32$), twelve historical summer Landsat scenes spanning 1994–2024 were sampled at the WGS84 coordinates for Chapin Court (45.33286, -122.61601), Rivercrest Park (45.34510, -122.60766), and Hillendale Park (45.32901, -122.59703). Surface temperatures at Chapin Court exceeded the two-control average on all twelve sampled dates (+7.02°F to +17.31°F), and this project's computational pipeline reproduced every published Chapin Court Land Surface Temperature (LST), every two-control average, and every calculated contrast within ±0.005°F (all 36 QA_PIXEL samples clear, bits 0–5 unset; QA_RADSAT = 0).

Methodological Scope, Native Sensor Resolution & Weather Provenance (Class A-R): Three methodological constraints govern interpretation of Table 3. First, Class A-R denotes internal computational reproduction by this project's scripts from archived USGS Collection 2 Level-2 rasters; the analysis has not been independently replicated by an outside party. Second, because Landsat thermal infrared sensors (Landsat 5 TM Band 6 at 120 m, Landsat 7 ETM+ Band 6 at 60 m, and Landsat 8/9 TIRS Band 10 at 100 m) have a native spatial resolution of 100–120 m (60–120 m across sensors) that is cubic-convolution resampled by USGS onto a 30 m product grid, adjacent 30 m pixels are not independent measurements and cannot support parcel-scale attribution. Third, as a methodological provenance reconciliation (Class A-R), comparing the published Ambient column against Open-Meteo historical series established that the published values match best_match local-calendar-day maxima within MAE = 0.058°F (RMSE = 0.076°F), whereas interpolated air temperatures at the exact ~18:45 UTC (~10:45–11:56 AM local) satellite overpass are ~9.44°F cooler; accordingly, the published Ambient column reflects daily-high air temperature rather than overpass-hour air temperature.

3.2 Spatial-Buffer Sensitivity (324 Pixels) & Preserved Class C Limitations / Counter-Evidence Class A-R (3×3 Buffer) · Class C (Unresolved & Counter-Evidence)

Across Cycles 2 and 3, the following sensitivity and counter-evidence findings are preserved to prevent overclaiming:

  • Centered 3×3 (90×90 m) Spatial-Buffer Test (Class A-R): All 324/324 pixels (12 scenes × 3 sites × 9 resampled 30 m pixels) passed clear-sky and saturation screening. Using 3×3 window means, Chapin Court remains warmer than the two-control average on 12 of 12 scenes (range +7.74°F to +16.19°F), with mean excess of +10.48°F across the 7 normal summer scenes and +12.08°F across the 5 high-heat scenes. Because a 90×90 m window spans roughly the footprint of a single native 100–120 m thermal pixel, this test checks local grid-alignment sensitivity rather than independent multi-pixel replication.
  • Individual Control Values on 9 of 12 Scenes (Class C): While the two-control average reproduced on all 12 scenes, individual Rivercrest and Hillendale control-pixel values on 9 of 12 scenes exhibit equal-and-opposite unreconciled offsets of up to ~4°F (±0.16°F to ±3.98°F) relative to the originally published table.
  • Tree Removal Caused the Heating (Class C — Not Supported by Current Data): Unadjusted epoch means of the surface-temperature contrast are +12.81°F pre-2009 ($n=5$), +10.00°F during 2009–2018 ($n=4$), and +12.38°F post-2018 ($n=3$), and the single largest contrast in the series occurred on July 24, 2006 (+17.31°F)—predating the 2009–2018 period. In addition, parcel/corridor tree-removal dates remain unverified in primary municipal permit records.
  • Heatwave Amplification (Class C — Statistically Non-Significant): Comparisons between high-heat and normal-summer regimes do not establish statistically significant heatwave amplification of the Chapin–control surface-temperature contrast across tested specifications (p ≈ 0.27 / 0.13 / 0.42).
  • AQI 91 Screenshot vs. Modeled AQI 79 (Class C — Unauthenticated): While the November 1, 2026 (08:45 PST / 16:45 UTC) consumer map screenshot displays a localized US AQI 91 value over Oregon City, the screenshot is not independently authenticated, and Cycle 3 Open-Meteo/CAMS global reanalysis models Oregon City/Chapin at US AQI 79 (PM2.5 = 23.15 µg/m³, boundary-layer height 151.25 m, 10 m wind 0.865 m/s), with McMinnville modeled higher at AQI 83 and Newberg at AQI 79. Neither unique Oregon City particulate trapping nor a verified temperature inversion is established.

4.1 Individualized Forbearance vs. Complaint-Driven Enforcement (Class B) & Ordinance 26-1007 (Class A-V) Class B (Forbearance Comparison) · Class A-V (Ordinance Text)

Because this comparison involves different property types (commercial/promenade vs. residential parcels) and the underlying comparator enforcement log has not yet been inspected, it is classified as Class B (not yet established as a pattern).
