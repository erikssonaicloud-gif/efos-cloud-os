THE HOMESTEADER THESIS

The Full Circle of Real Property Law: Physical Stewardship vs. Paper Abstraction

In the 19th-century American West, homesteaders who cleared timber, dug wells, and built dwellings repeatedly confronted a structural legal threat: absentee speculators and railroad syndicates wielding defective land scrip, robo-signed patent transfers, and distant capital leverage to eject physical occupants. Early state legislatures and courts responded by enacting strict local county recording acts, requiring public chain-of-title transparency and physical possession of negotiable instruments before an occupant could be dispossessed.

A century and a half later, real property law has come full circle. During the residential mortgage backed securities (RMBS) boom, Wall Street originators bypassed county recording systems via MERS, split promissory notes from deeds of trust, lost original instruments between warehouse lenders and securitized trusts (CWALT, CWABS), and relied on high-volume default mills to push unrepresented homeowners through summary dockets.

“Property law rules that once balanced the rights of mortgagors and mortgagees now completely fail to furnish aggrieved homeowners with meaningful relief when faced with wrongs that stem from the complexities of the securitization of mortgage loans and the acts of intermediaries.” — Prof. Christopher K. Odinet, Modernizing Mortgage Law, 100 N.C. L. Rev. 89 (2021)
COMPUTATIONAL FORENSICS

How the Retrospective Justice Engine Audits a Cold or Clouded Docket

Traditional legal triage assumes that once a foreclosure judgment or bankruptcy dismissal is entered, the factual record is closed. The PALADIN Retrospective Justice Engine operates on the opposite premise: under black-letter Oregon authority (Portland Gen. Elec. Co. v. Ebasco Servs., Inc., 353 Or 856; Wells Fargo Bank, N.A. v. Jasper, 289 Or App 617; ORCP 71 B(1)(d) & 71 C), a judgment procured without personal jurisdiction or through undisclosed attorney conflicts and extrinsic fraud is void, carries no time limitation, and cannot ripen into validity through the passage of time.

PALADIN ingests multi-decade dockets—county land records, PACER bankruptcy claims, SEC pooling and servicing agreements, remittance reports, and municipal enforcement logs—to build a deterministic, page-linked chronology that separates internet foreclosure folklore from admissible statutory defects.

Section 02 • Primary Legal Scholarship & Research Notes

Excerpts of Iniquity, Chicago Gentrification & Broken-Windows Nuisance Abatement

A central finding of post-2008 legal scholarship is that the foreclosure crisis did not end at the courthouse steps. Instead, a two-stage institutional pipeline emerged: Stage 1 occurred inside overloaded state foreclosure dockets, where courts, bar associations, and triaged legal-aid clinics treated complex securitization and servicing defects as “indefensible” borrower defaults; Stage 2 unfolded in municipal code-enforcement and administrative building courts, where clouded titles, zombie foreclosures, and utility shutoffs were weaponized to strip equity and accelerate neighborhood displacement.

FORECLOSURE COURT TRIAGE • DEPAUL J. SOC. JUST.

Kelli Dudley — An Excerpt of Iniquity: How Court Systems, Attorneys, and Legal Aid Organizations Cheated Homeowners in Foreclosure (2019)

Written by veteran Chicago foreclosure defense and civil rights attorney Kelli Dudley (12 DePaul Journal for Social Justice), this landmark article dismantles the pervasive institutional myth of the “indefensible foreclosure.” Dudley documents how special foreclosure Rocket Dockets, unexamined hearsay affidavits, and grant-dependent legal-aid triage protocols systematically screened out meritorious defenses involving standing, fabricated endorsements, servicer misapplication of payments, and Truth in Lending / RESPA violations.

“Homeowners were told their cases were indefensible not because the banks could satisfy the rules of evidence, but because the institutional apparatus—courts, bar associations, and legal aid intake screens—refused to require banks to obey the rules of civil procedure.” — Kelli Dudley, An Excerpt of Iniquity (2019), synthesized in PALADIN Research Notes

Emma Chmiel and forensic audits by McDonnell Property Analytics expose the institutional resistance faced by litigants who uncover systemic servicing and title-chain fraud.

MUNICIPAL COURTS & GENTRIFICATION

Kelli Dudley — Without Due Process of Law: Deprivation and Gentrification in Chicago

In her companion study of Chicago’s Housing and Building Court apparatus, Dudley traces what happens to properties after mortgage servicing breakdowns or economic shocks leave homeowners financially strained. Rather than providing infrastructure repair assistance, municipalities deploy fast-track administrative building and nuisance dockets that strip procedural safeguards, impose compounding daily fines, order utility disconnections or summary vacates, and appoint private receivers or demolition contractors.

“Municipal building and nuisance enforcement operates as an engine of involuntary title transfer—converting temporary infrastructure distress or title-clouded maintenance delays into permanent dispossession and developer gentrification without constitutional due process.” — Research Synthesis: Without Due Process of Law (Dudley) & DLCD Div. 47 Rulemaking Record

CODE ENFORCEMENT FORENSICS

Shane Diller — Is Nuisance Abatement Broken Windows Policing?

Examines how modern municipal nuisance-abatement ordinances and code-enforcement divisions function as a civil, property-targeted analog to “broken windows” policing. Because civil nuisance proceedings lack Sixth Amendment counsel guarantees and often operate through administrative notices or summary abatement liens, code-enforcement officers exercise sweeping discretion over who receives extensions versus who faces utility termination, placarding, and armed police accompaniment.

“When municipal code enforcement treats physical signs of financial or title distress as public nuisances to be abated through punitive fines and forced vacates, it criminalizes poverty while bypassing the constitutional checks of criminal procedure.” — Research Synthesis: Shane Diller, Is Nuisance Abatement Broken Windows Policing

3.1 The Institutional Side-Switching Paradox: From Arguing Brandrup v. ReconTrust & Defending the Baron Portfolio to Prosecuting Foreclosures Against the Same Client

One of the most striking institutional findings in the 6-sheet Master Tracker is the documented trajectory of attorney Jeffrey A. Myers (OSB #094561). In 2013, Myers argued and won the landmark Oregon Supreme Court decision Brandrup v. ReconTrust Co., 353 Or 668 (2013), establishing that Mortgage Electronic Registration Systems (MERS) is neither a statutory beneficiary under the Oregon Trust Deed Act nor eligible to hold or transfer legal title to a trust deed without proof of agency from the note owner.

From 2011 through 2016 (Phase 1), while practicing at Bowles Fernandez Law LLC (which simultaneously served as registered agent for Turning Leaf Homes, LLC), Myers served as company and litigation defense counsel for the Baron portfolio entities (Big Blue Capital Partners, Turning Leaf Homes, LLC, Turning Leaf Homes III, LLC, and RenX Group, LLC) across state and federal courts—including helping Turning Leaf Homes III acquire the distressed residential parcel at 12054 Chapin Ct., Oregon City out of the Garboden Chapter 7 bankruptcy estate (15-35133-rld7). In those cases, Myers repeatedly pleaded two signature affirmative defenses and counterclaims against foreclosing banks: (1) that the securitized trust plaintiff was not the real party in interest due to defective MERS assignments under Brandrup, and (2) an affirmative Counterclaim for Accounting and Right of Redemption.

In 2017 (Phase 2), Myers transitioned to the institutional foreclosure firm ZBS Law, LLP (formerly Zieve, Brodnax & Steele). Without disclosing his prior attorney-client relationship to the court or obtaining informed consent under ORPC 1.9(a), Myers initiated and prosecuted judicial foreclosures on behalf of The Bank of New York Mellon and U.S. Bank N.A. directly against his own former client (Turning Leaf Homes / Fite, LLC)—including on the exact same Oregon City property (17CV38591, 12054 Chapin Ct.) he had assisted TLH in acquiring. In 17CV38591, Myers moved for summary judgment to dismiss TLH’s identical Accounting/Redemption counterclaim and suppressed the fact that the 2012 MERS assignment ran to a different securitized trust (CWALT 2006-OC11) than his named plaintiff (CWALT 2006-OC10)—the precise defect governed by Brandrup.

USBC DISTRICT OF OREGON • CHAPTER 11 COLLAPSE WAVE

3.2 The 2016–2018 Bankruptcy Wave & U.S. Trustee Refiling Bar

As institutional lenders accelerated judicial foreclosures against the Baron portfolio between 2016 and 2018, the portfolio entities filed a sequence of Chapter 11 petitions in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Oregon: 16-32747-tmb11 (Crimson Investment Group), 17-30353-tmb11 (Turning Leaf Homes IV), 17-31944-tmb11 (Turning Leaf Homes V), 17-33139-tmb11 (RenX Group II), and finally 18-30038-tmb11 (Fite, LLC).

On January 3, 2018—two days before filing 18-30038-tmb11—Turning Leaf Homes, LLC, TLH IV, and TLH V executed Articles of Merger into newly formed Fite, LLC, consolidating roughly 20 encumbered Oregon properties into a single bankruptcy estate. U.S. Trustee trial attorney Carla McClurg initiated an intensive compliance audit in February–March 2018 (documented in Sheet 5 of the Master Workbook), demanding property-by-property rent rolls, security-deposit accounting, and insurance proof. On April 30, 2018, the Bankruptcy Court dismissed 18-30038-tmb11 with prejudice and imposed a bar on refiling, followed by administrative dissolution of Fite, LLC on October 11, 2018.

DOWNSTREAM MUNICIPAL IMPACT • ZOMBIE FORECLOSURES

3.3 How Portfolio Collapse + Unrecorded Sheriff’s Deeds Create Municipal Traps

When Fite, LLC collapsed in 2018 and its state-court counsel withdrew across pending foreclosure dockets, institutional plaintiffs obtained summary judgments on unopposed records. Yet in multiple instances, the foreclosing trusts and their servicers (Shellpoint / NewRez) failed to complete clean title transfers:

  • Unrecorded Post-Auction Deeds: Even after conducting a credit-bid Sheriff’s Sale (such as the November 1, 2023 auction in 17CV38591), no Sheriff’s Deed was recorded in the county land records (ORS 18.960, ORS 93.640) after the 180-day redemption window expired.
  • Dual-Track Servicer Billing: Months after claiming to have auctioned the property, the servicer continued issuing monthly mortgage statements billing active principal, interest, and force-placed hazard insurance.
  • The Municipal Permit & Utility Lockout: Because the title remains clouded between a void/unrecorded foreclosure and a dissolved predecessor portfolio, municipal building departments and state weatherization programs refuse to process standard owner-occupant repair permits—setting the stage for punitive code enforcement.
