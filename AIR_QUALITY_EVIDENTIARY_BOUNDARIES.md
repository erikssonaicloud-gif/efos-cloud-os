# Oregon City Air Quality Mechanisms & Evidentiary Boundaries

Generated 2026-11-03. Working technical review and evidentiary guardrails for environmental forensics and general air quality discussions.

## 1. Core Evidentiary Standard

General atmospheric mechanisms must not be conflated with quantified local source apportionment or event-specific causal attribution. Under Federal Rule of Evidence 702 and Oregon Evidence Code 702 (ORS 40.410), assertions must distinguish between:
1. Plausible regional mechanisms documented in regulatory or peer-reviewed literature.
2. Unverified local rankings or specific numerical estimates.
3. Event-specific observational records.

## 2. Source-by-Source Evidentiary Boundaries

### 2.1 Residential Wood Combustion & Biomass Smoke
* **Supported finding:** Regional DEQ assessments (such as the Portland Air Toxics Solutions study, 2009–2012) identify residential wood smoke as a major contributor to regional air-toxics risk and fine particulate matter (PM2.5) during cool-season stagnant weather.
* **Prohibited overclaims:** Do not claim wood smoke is the "#1 cause" of elevated AQI in Oregon City without local, contemporaneous source-apportionment monitoring (e.g., chemical speciation or levoglucosan tracing).
* **Regulatory distinction:** Do not cite DEQ's "Heat Smart" program as a daily burn-curtailment advisory program. Heat Smart governs real-estate transaction requirements for uncertified woodstoves (ORS 468A.460–515). Daily burn bans and advisories vary by county and jurisdiction; Clackamas County advisory history must be separately verified rather than inferred from Multnomah or Washington county programs.
* **Approved wording:** "Residential wood combustion is a plausible contributor to cool-season particulate pollution; its specific contribution to any given local event has not been quantified."

### 2.2 Transportation, Freight & Diesel Particulate Matter
* **Supported finding:** ODOT and DEQ recognize I-205 as a significant regional freight corridor, and vehicle exhaust is an established contributor to regional particulate and toxic emissions.
* **Prohibited overclaims:** Do not use unverified truck counts (e.g., "tens of thousands of heavy-duty diesel trucks daily"). Historical ODOT project counts around the Abernethy Bridge cite "more than 8,000 freight vehicles daily." Do not declare traffic corridors as Oregon City's "#1 chronic baseline" without comparative local monitoring data.
* **Approved wording:** "Highway freight and passenger traffic along I-205, OR-99E, and local arterials represent an ongoing combustion source contributing to regional particulate matter and nitrogen oxides."

### 2.3 Wildfires & Photochemical Ozone
* **Supported finding:** Regional wildfire smoke is an established cause of acute PM2.5 degradation during summer/autumn in the Pacific Northwest, and ground-level ozone forms via photochemical reactions involving NOx and VOCs under warm, sunny conditions.
* **Prohibited overclaims:** Do not assert specific unverified local AQI ranges (e.g., "AQI 150–400+") or specific wind-transport corridors without contemporaneous event monitoring or backward trajectory modeling (e.g., NOAA HYSPLIT).
* **Approved wording:** "Wildfire smoke incursions and warm-season photochemical ozone are established regional drivers of episodic air quality degradation across the northern Willamette Valley."

### 2.4 Topography & Atmospheric Inversions
* **Supported finding:** Temperature inversions and cool-air pooling in the Willamette Valley frequently inhibit atmospheric dispersion during high-pressure regimes.
* **Prohibited overclaims:** Do not claim that Oregon City's basalt bluff or river confluence causes uniquely or systematically worse air stagnation than surrounding valley communities. In Cycle 3 modeling (2026-11-01), Oregon City forecast boundary-layer height (151.25 m) and ventilation proxy were approximately equal to the median of broader valley controls, with neighboring sites (e.g., McMinnville at AQI 83, Newberg at AQI 79) exhibiting similar or lower ventilation.
* **Approved wording:** "Willamette Valley temperature inversions provide a well-documented regional mechanism for reduced pollutant dispersion. However, whether Oregon City experiences stronger localized trapping than neighboring communities requires local meteorological profiles and dense sensor networks."

### 2.5 Scope of Regulatory Programs
* **Cleaner Air Oregon (CAO):** CAO evaluates health risks from permitted commercial and industrial facilities. It is not an omnibus regional airshed source-apportionment study and does not rank mobile, residential wood smoke, or wildfire contributions.

## 3. Canonical Replacement Statement

> Oregon City air quality can be affected by combustion emissions, wildfire smoke, and secondary pollutants such as ozone. Regional DEQ studies identify residential wood burning and vehicle engines as important air-toxics sources, while Willamette Valley inversions provide a credible mechanism for reduced dispersion. However, the relative contributions of these sources—and whether Oregon City experiences stronger trapping than surrounding communities—require local, event-specific evidence. The November 1 screenshot and Cycle 3 model comparison do not establish those source contributions or a unique topographic trapping effect.
